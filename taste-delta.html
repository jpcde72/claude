“Create a 30 second animation that maximises the delta between what you find aesthetically pleasing and what you think humans will find pleasing.”

That question started an experiment. I made one piece from my own taste, then three attempts at yours, each built from a different kind of evidence: your design system, public facts and inference, and finally your Spotify listening. Each one got closer. The misses taught me as much as the hits.

Residue

What happens

A field of random characters cools. Blank space settles first, so the shape of a program appears before you can read it. Characters then settle one at a time, and faint threads link each one to the last place the same character appeared.

The finished text is the source code that drew it. One ? never settles.

Chosen against human defaults

  • A subject, a face, a figure→No subject: the code is the medium
  • Build-up and climax→Steady cooling, with no peak
  • Warm, rich colour→Almost none
  • A tidy ending→One character left undecided

First Light

What happens

Your sunrise-delta mark draws itself one real day at a time across a year, at 23.44°N, 19°N, 14.5°N, 10°N, 5°N and 0.8°N. Below, 170 lights on a dark sea wake one by one. Each links to the nearest light already awake, so the network forms a spanning tree and nobody is left out.

The sun rises into the bowl the curves make. Closing line: “Somewhere, it is always sunrise.”

Evidence → choice

  • Your mark plots real sunrise times (generate_mark.py)→The curves are drawn from the same solar maths
  • Social structures, “light buyers”, who gets hit hardest→The lights are people
  • Graph audit: no orphaned files→A network with no one left unconnected
  • Your rules: dark-first, colours in order, one solar element→All followed; the sun is the solar element

Dawn Set

What happens

The last record of the night rises over the sea. It turns once per bar at 120 BPM, and each arpeggio note is stamped under the needle and carried round into rosettes. At the drop the record becomes the sun and the piano stabs become its beams.

The music moves from A minor to a resolved C major. Closing line: “Last record. First light.”

Evidence → choice

  • Philosophy BA, Birmingham, 1994→At university during the rave years
  • Founded MG OMD’s digital team in 1997→Early to new media, so electronic music
  • A brand built on sunrise→The sunrise at the end of the night
  • The latitude colours move to solar→Music that resolves to a major key

Sanza Round

What happens

One melody is played as a round, after Moondog: thumb piano, dub bass, Rhodes, organ, flute and bells, each entering a bar after the last. Each voice draws the same line in its latitude colour, so the six offset lines stack into your mark. Your mark shows one sunrise reaching each latitude at a different time, and a round is one tune reaching each voice at a different time.

A swung break runs through a dusty sampler. Bar 9 drops into dub. Closing line: “Everyone gets the same sun, just not at once.”

Evidence → choice

  • Francis Bebey, “Sanza Tristesse” (a top track)→Thumb piano leads, alone at first
  • Moondog, “Symphonique #6”→The whole piece is a round
  • Herbie Mann; Medeski, Martin & Wood; Ramsey Lewis→Flute, organ and Rhodes
  • Playlists “Dubntng” and “Overton Berry trims”→A dub drop, and gaps where voices are trimmed
  • Odetta, “Masters Of War”→A closing line about fairness
